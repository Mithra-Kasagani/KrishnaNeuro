# Medical Content Governance Policy

**Website:** Krishna Neuro Psychiatric Centre  
**Applies to:** English and Telugu condition guides, articles, service explanations, FAQs, emergency guidance and medical metadata  
**Policy version:** 20 August 2026

## 1. Purpose

This policy keeps website content useful, medically responsible, current and transparent. Search visibility never overrides patient safety, factual accuracy, privacy or professional ethics.

## 3. Required workflow

1. Define the patient or caregiver question the page answers.
2. Draft in original, plain language; do not copy another publisher.
3. Check claims against current authoritative sources.
4. Check emergency, medication, pregnancy, child, older-adult and substance-withdrawal safety wording where relevant.
5. Check for stigmatizing, fear-based, absolute or promotional language.
6. Add internal links only when they help the reader take a logical next step.
7. Record the author/editor, sources and actual content-update date.
8. Obtain medical review for clinical claims before enabling reviewer UI/schema.
9. Obtain separate Telugu review for translated/localized clinical content.
10. Publish, test metadata/schema/links and retain an approval record outside the public repository.

## 4. Source standard

Prefer current primary or authoritative sources:

- World Health Organization (WHO)
- Government of India / Ministry of Health and Family Welfare
- NIMHANS
- National Institute of Mental Health (NIMH)
- National Institute for Health and Care Excellence (NICE)
- NHS
- recognized professional guidelines
- peer-reviewed systematic reviews or major clinical guidelines when needed

A source link supports a claim; it must not be copied. Summaries must be written independently and preserve nuance, uncertainty and applicability limits.

## 5. Review frequency and update triggers

### Target review cycle

- Emergency, suicide, withdrawal and medication-safety pages: review at least every 6 months.
- Other condition and treatment pages: review at least every 12 months.
- Legal/privacy and appointment-flow pages: review when data handling or applicable requirements change.
- Contact, hours and location information: verify whenever clinic operations change and at least quarterly.

These are governance targets, not claims that a review has already occurred.

### Review immediately when

- a major guideline or safety warning changes;
- emergency contact information changes;
- the clinic changes services, hours, address, phone or appointment handling;
- a factual error or harmful ambiguity is reported;
- Search Console shows a page ranking for an intent it does not safely answer;
- translated wording is found to change clinical meaning.

Do not change `updatedAt` or `reviewedAt` merely to make content appear fresh.

## 6. Required page elements

Clinical condition/article pages should include, where appropriate:

- clear purpose and plain-language definition;
- symptoms and functional impact;
- assessment and treatment overview;
- when to seek professional or emergency help;
- patient/family guidance;
- sources and further reading;
- visible author/editor and actual update date;
- medical reviewer and real review date only after approval;
- educational-information disclaimer;
- relevant doctor, service and appointment links.

## 7. Prohibited claims and practices

Never publish or mark up:

- “best,” “No.1,” guaranteed cure/recovery or unsupported superiority claims;
- fabricated reviews, ratings, patient stories, outcomes or statistics;
- invented credentials, registration numbers, memberships, awards, affiliations or experience duration;
- hidden keywords, doorway pages or repetitive city pages;
- individualized diagnosis or prescribing advice;
- advice to start, share, reduce or abruptly stop medication without clinical guidance;
- review schema for copied, composite, anonymous or unverified testimonials;
- a medical-review date that is not supported by an actual review.

## 8. Terminology and tone

- Use person-first, non-stigmatizing language.
- Explain technical terms at first use.
- Distinguish symptoms, syndromes, diagnoses and risk factors.
- State uncertainty when evidence or individual applicability varies.
- Avoid blaming patients, parents or families.
- Avoid using crisis language as a conversion tactic.
- Explain that assessment does not automatically mean medication.

## 9. Corrections

Potentially harmful errors should be corrected promptly. Record:

- page URL;
- issue reported;
- source checked;
- exact correction;
- editor;
- reviewer when clinical;
- correction date.

Material corrections should update `updatedAt`. Reviewer information changes only after a fresh documented review.

## 10. Search and analytics governance

- Use Search Console data to improve existing useful pages before creating new ones.
- Prioritize pages with high impressions, positions 5–20, declining clicks, weak CTR or poor conversion paths.
- Do not send names, phone numbers, symptoms, diagnoses, medicines, records or private messages to analytics.
- Track only safe events such as call, WhatsApp, directions, contact, appointment start and appointment submit.
- Search performance, backlinks, Maps visibility and AI citations must be reported as external observations, never guaranteed outcomes.
